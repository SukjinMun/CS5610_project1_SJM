This page runs the 2D Ising model, a lattice of magnetic spins, close to the spin models and magnetic structures in my research.

2D Ising model

The Ising model is the simplest picture of a magnet. Each square in the grid is a tiny magnet, called a spin, that points either up or down.

Neighboring spins prefer to point the same way, while heat flips them at random. At low temperature the spins line up into large patches of one color, and at high temperature they stay a random mix. The change happens near a temperature of 2.27.

The simulation uses the Metropolis algorithm. It picks spins at random and flips each one if that does not raise the energy, and otherwise with a chance that grows with temperature. Magnetization is the average spin, from -1 to 1. Energy per spin is -2 when every neighbor agrees and near 0 for a random grid.

Tc

Temperature is shown as kBT/J, and energy per spin is measured in units of J, with ferromagnetic coupling and no external field. For an infinite square lattice at equilibrium, spontaneous magnetization vanishes at Tc ≈ 2.269. These finite lattices fluctuate and need time to equilibrate, so the displayed magnetization need not be zero there.
