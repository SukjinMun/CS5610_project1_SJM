Research

I build machine learning methods for materials discovery and characterization, drawing on earlier work in X-ray and neutron diffraction.

Research interests

  • Machine learning methods that accelerate materials discovery and characterization
  • Generative models for atomistic structures
  • Machine-learned interatomic potentials and DFT screening of 2D materials
  • Powder XRD and neutron diffraction analysis

Bragg peak explorer

A crystal diffracts X-rays only at certain angles, set by the spacing of its atomic planes. Choose a cubic lattice, a lattice constant a, and an X-ray source to see where the peaks of a powder pattern fall.

Each peak follows Bragg's law, λ = 2d sin θ, with d = a / √(h² + k² + l²) for the planes hkl. Body centered lattices keep only reflections with h + k + l even. Face centered lattices keep those with h, k, l all even or all odd.

The sticks mark every peak between 10° and 90° in 2θ, and the first ones carry their hkl labels. All sticks have the same height because the explorer computes positions, not intensities. The default, face centered cubic with a = 4.05 Å, matches aluminum.
